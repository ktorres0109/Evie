01
Product design thesis
238 words

Most cycle apps are built to be opened often. Evie is built to be trusted — which is a slower, quieter thing.

The person who logs owns the data. Not a server, not a partner, not us. Everything meaningful works with the radio off: the estimate, the calendar, the report you hand a doctor, the lock on the door. There is no account to create and nothing to sell, so there is nothing to optimize for except being right and being clear.

Being right means showing our work. Evie never gives a single confident date where it has a range, never calls a day safe, and never lets color alone carry a health signal. Every estimate can be opened to see what it was built from, what was excluded, and how much data it actually had. Uncertainty is a feature of the interface, not an apology in a footnote.

A partner may be invited, once, from across the room — a code shown on one phone and confirmed on the other, no relay in between. They see only what was ticked, phrased as care rather than diagnosis, and the owner can pause it in a tap.

The reward for showing up is a plant that has grown. Not a streak, not a number. Miss a week and it is still there, and so are you.

Screen map

Two apps in one binary. The owner gets five destinations; the partner gets four and can reach nothing else. The partner role is not a permission level on the owner's app — it is a separate, smaller shell chosen at first launch and never switchable without re-pairing.

Scope for this pass: core cycle tracking and the optional fertility-awareness mode. TTC, pregnancy, perimenopause, the assistant, reminders and reports exist in the codebase and are marked below as adjacent, unspecified surfaces.

Scope change from the repo roadmap

Partner sharing is listed as explicitly excluded in docs/CURRENT_PROGRESS_AND_ROADMAP.md, on the grounds that it needs a peer or relay. This spec reinstates it in the one form that needs neither: a local, same-room pairing over a short-lived code, with the shared payload written to the partner's device and refreshed only when the two are on the same network. No Evie server exists at any point.

Wireframe — owner onboarding

Six steps. Each screen is a title, one choice and a button — nothing to read before you can act.

1 · Privacy — “No account. Nothing leaves this phone.” Then Continue.
2 · Purpose (shown) — four tiles, pick any, or none.
3 · Last period — a date, or “Not sure”.
4 · Birth control — skippable. Hormonal methods pause fertility estimates, and the screen says so.
5 · Reminders — pick a tone first; the system prompt comes after.
6 · App lock — offered, not required.
Copy budget

Title up to 5 words, helper up to 4, no paragraphs. Partner sharing never appears here — it lives in You.

Wireframe — owner Today

Six blocks, fixed order, no feed. Everything above the quick-log row is reachable with a thumb on a 6.1″ phone; nothing below it is required.

Cycle-day hero
The largest type on the screen. “of about 29” carries the honesty — the denominator is an estimate too. Phase line is descriptive of the body, never of the person: mid-cycle, not you may feel.
Prediction card
A range, always. The countdown pill says “in 12 days” only because the range starts then; when the range is wide it reads “in about two weeks”. The confidence meter and the Why this estimate? link are part of the card, not buried in settings.
Quick-log row
Five 48px targets. Four are the user's most-used categories, learned from their own history; the fifth is always More. Tapping opens a half sheet, commits on selection, and closes — a full check-in is four taps and about twelve seconds.
Fertility card — conditional
Present only if fertility-awareness mode is on. Absent entirely otherwise; it does not appear greyed out or as an upsell.
Insight — one, or none
Requires a stated evidence threshold (here: 8 comparable days). Below threshold the block is replaced by a calm empty state — “Not enough yet to say anything useful” — never by filler.
Companion
Counts check-ins, never consecutive days. The phrasing is observational: a leaf opened. There is no number to protect and nothing to break.
Bleeding
Hero becomes “Day 2 of your period”, berry accent, prediction card swaps to “Usually 4–6 days”.
Late
“3 days past the estimated window.” No alarm colour, no pregnancy prompt unless TTC mode is on.
Suppressed
Hormonal method logged: prediction card states the estimate is withheld and links to why.
No history
Hero reads “Let's start where you are.” Quick-log row is full width and the only action.

Wireframe — logging, under 30 seconds

One scrolling surface, ordered by how often each field is touched. Nothing is a modal. Every control writes immediately — the button at the bottom dismisses, it does not save.

Flow — six discrete steps
None / spotting / light / medium / heavy / very heavy as labelled radios. Not a slider: a slider implies a continuum the data does not have, and cannot be hit accurately with a thumb. Selecting anything but None on a day outside a known period opens a single follow-up: Is this the start of your period?
Symptom chips — presets, then depth
The first seven are the user's own most-logged, reordered monthly and never mid-session. Long-press any chip to add severity and impact; a plain tap stays a plain tap. The count beside the label is the whole summary.
Mood — words, not faces
Six plain words, multi-select, no 1–10 scale. Energy and sleep are not tracked: they add taps and improve no estimate.
Intimacy and contraception
Behind a row, not on the surface — so a shoulder-glance at the log screen reveals nothing. Inside: multi-select activity, protection used, and method, with no framing of frequency and no “goal”.
BBT and OPK — advanced, always visible
Present as empty affordances even when unused, so the advanced path is discoverable without a settings hunt. BBT accepts °C or °F with the unit as a tap, and flags a reading more than 1.5° from the user's own range rather than a population range.
Save feedback
A persistent inline strip, not a toast that vanishes. It names the location — on this device — because that is the product promise, and it says offline plainly rather than showing a sync spinner that will never resolve.
The 30-second path

Today → tap Flow → tap None → sheet closes → tap Mood → tap two chips → back. Six taps, no scrolling, no confirmation. The full screen above is for the days someone wants to say more.

Wireframe — calendar

One month, one screen, no horizontal scroll and no pinch. Cells are 46px with a 4px gutter — on a 360dp device that is 44px, still above target, and the row height never collapses to fit a sixth week.

Solid versus dashed
The single most important visual rule in the product. A filled cell is a fact the user entered. A dashed outline is the app's guess. The distinction is a border style, so it survives greyscale, high contrast and colour-blind rendering intact.
Prediction is a window
Four dashed cells, not one highlighted date. The card below the grid states the width in words and why it is that wide. As history accumulates the window narrows visibly, which is the only progress indicator the product needs.
Legend is permanent
Not a tooltip, not an onboarding coach mark. It sits under the grid on every visit, because a calendar whose symbols must be remembered is a calendar that will be misread.
Day sheet
Tapping any cell — past or future — opens a half sheet with that day's entries, fully editable, plus a delete. Editing a past period start recalculates and the sheet says so before it closes: this changes your next estimate.
Fertility overlay
When the advanced mode is on, a second row of hatched / stippled / flat markers appears beneath the date inside each cell — pattern first, colour second, and the legend grows to match. It is a toggle in the ⓘ menu, off by default even for mode users.
Performance note

Month navigation is a swap, not a carousel — the outgoing month is removed and the incoming one painted, with a 180 ms opacity crossfade. No off-screen months are kept mounted, so memory stays flat across a year of scrubbing on a 3 GB device.

Wireframe — the estimate explanation

This screen is the product's conscience. It exists so that no number elsewhere in the app has to be taken on faith, and it is reachable in one tap from every place an estimate is shown.

Answer first
The range restated at the top, at display size, so arriving here never feels like a punishment for asking. Confidence is a countable meter with a word beside it — three of five, not a percentage the model cannot honestly produce.
Inputs, itemised
Every cycle that fed the number, with its own length. The user can check the arithmetic. Nothing is aggregated away.
Exclusions are visible, with reasons
The hardest rule and the most important: data the engine discarded gets its own labelled section, not a footnote. And the copy makes clear the cycle was not deleted or judged — it is still in the history and still in the trends. A user who sees a 52-day cycle silently vanish loses trust permanently.
Method in one sentence
Plain language above the two numbers that produced the range. No formula notation, no model name, no citation wall — those live in Insights → Methodology for anyone who wants them.
Sufficiency, without a chore
States what more data would buy, then immediately states the ceiling. It does not turn accuracy into a task the user has failed at, and there is no “log more to unlock” framing anywhere.
Suppressed
Hormonal method logged: the whole screen becomes an explanation of why no estimate is offered, with the method and start date named.
Insufficient
Fewer than two complete cycles: no range at all. “We would rather say nothing than say something shaped like a fact.”
Irregular
Spread over ~7 days: confidence drops to one of five and the range is shown in weeks, not dates.

Wireframe — fertility-awareness mode

Off by default, even for users who chose fertility awareness at onboarding — that choice only makes the mode offerable. Enabling it requires a two-screen explanation and an explicit acknowledgement that this is not contraception.

Three states, never two
There is no binary. Unknown is a first-class answer and is shown far more often than users expect — any cycle with a gap, a wide spread, a recent method change, postpartum status or fewer than three comparable cycles resolves to it.
Forbidden vocabulary
Safe · protected · green light · clear · free · infertile · can't get pregnant. These are lint-checked in the string catalogue, not left to reviewer judgement. Teal is never named as a colour in copy and never appears without its full label.
The disclaimer is a component, not a footer
It sits second on the screen, at body size, in a bordered surface — above the evidence and above the inputs. It is present in every state, including Lower estimated fertility, where the temptation to soften it is strongest.
Inputs are labelled with their limits
OPK reads suggestive only in the row itself; BBT is described as retrospective — it confirms ovulation after the fact and cannot predict it. Neither ever raises confidence to a level the cycle history alone would not support.
Suppression states
Hormonal contraception, pregnancy, and the first six months postpartum replace the status card entirely with a plain statement that no estimate is being made, and why. The three-state legend is hidden in these cases so it cannot be read as applying.
Turning it off is a first-class action
Beside the primary action, not buried in settings, and it takes effect immediately with no retention prompt and no “are you sure you want to lose…”.

Wireframe — pairing, locally

The repo is right that partner sharing needs a peer. It does not need a server. Pairing is a same-room handshake; afterwards the partner's phone holds its own copy of exactly the consented fields, refreshed opportunistically over the local network and stale-labelled when it cannot be.

1 · Owner generates
A six-character code and its QR, valid three minutes, single use. Both forms are shown because scanning fails on cracked screens and old cameras — and because reading a code aloud is sometimes the kinder interaction.
2 · Partner enters
On the partner phone this is the entire first-run experience: scan or type, nothing else. The partner never creates a profile and never logs anything.
3 · Owner confirms the device
The owner's phone shows the requesting device's name and a four-word verification phrase that must match what the partner sees. The owner taps Confirm. A correct code alone is never sufficient — this is the step that makes a shoulder-surfed code useless.
4 · Scopes, chosen before the handshake completes
Four independent switches, each with its consequence written underneath in the first person. Symptoms is off by default and, when enabled, opens a second screen where each symptom is ticked individually — there is no “all symptoms” shortcut.
The not-shared list
Rendered from the same constant on both devices, word for word, so the owner and the partner are looking at the same promise. It appears here, in the owner's settings, and on the partner's You screen.
Pause and revoke
A single switch at the top of the sharing settings pauses everything instantly; the partner's app shows “Sharing is paused” with no reason given and no notification sent, because a notification would make pausing socially expensive. Revoke additionally wipes the partner's cached copy on next contact and tells the owner plainly that it cannot wipe a screenshot.
Honest limitation to state in the UI

Because the partner's copy lives on the partner's phone, revocation is best-effort. The confirm dialog says exactly that, in one sentence, rather than implying a remote kill switch the architecture cannot provide.

Wireframe — partner Today

The partner app is a card, not a dashboard. Four destinations, nothing tappable that expands into data, no history, no charts, no numbers that could be tracked over time.

Words, not metrics
The hero is a sentence at display size. No cycle day, no percentage, no phase wheel. A partner who wants to know “where is she at” gets a phrase; a partner who wants to build a model of her body gets nothing to build with.
Softened range
“Around the 18th to the 21st”, deliberately vaguer than the owner's own card, and always with the reminder that it moves. The partner is never given a date to hold her to.
Support comes from her, verbatim
Every line in the support card was typed by the owner. The attribution line is not decorative — it is the mechanism that stops the partner reading generic advice as a report on her state. Evie generates no suggestions here, ever.
The anti-stereotype statement
Permanent, on the home screen, not in a help article. It is the one piece of copy in the partner app that is about the product's own limits, and it is there because this is precisely the surface where a cycle app does the most social damage.
Staleness is stated
Local-only sync means the copy can be days old. The footer always says when it last updated and over what — never a spinner, never a silent stale render. Beyond 72 hours the cards dim and a line reads “This may be out of date.”
Paused state
All cards are replaced by one line: “Sharing is paused.” No reason, no timestamp, no “ask her to resume” button. The partner app never gives the partner a way to apply pressure.

Wireframe — the plant, and coming back

A plant in a vessel: quiet, not a character, no face, no name, no voice. It grows with total check-ins and it never shrinks. This screen is what a user sees after thirty-one days away — the single moment where most trackers lose them.

Five stages, monotonic
Seedling → two leaves → four → full → flowering, at roughly 5 / 15 / 35 / 70 / 120 lifetime check-ins. The plant is never smaller than it was. There is no wilting, no drooping, no thirst meter, no state that reads as reproach.
Resting, not dying
After a gap the plant renders at slightly lower saturation and the pot loses its highlight. That is the entire absence treatment: a change in light, reversed by one check-in. Nothing brown, nothing bare.
The return copy
Three sentences, in order: you are welcome, nothing broke, here are your options — including the option to do nothing. “Just looking” is a real, unstyled third choice and dismisses to a normal Today. The number of days missed is never displayed.
The history strip
Ten weeks of activity as blocks. The empty stretch is shown plainly and captioned as unremarkable — because hiding it would imply it was shameful. The one grey block is the week partially logged. This is the closest the product comes to a streak, and it counts nothing.
What the plant is never used for
Gating an insight, unlocking a feature, a currency, a shop, a spin, a timer, a daily bonus, a comparison with anyone. Cosmetic pot and pigment variations unlock at stages and are purely optional; nothing about the health product changes.
Notification tone, chosen by the user
Warm (“Your plant has been sitting in the sun”), neutral (“Log today?”), minimal (period-window reminders only), or off. Default is neutral. No notification ever names a symptom, a phase or the word period on a lock screen.
Rendered how

Five flat vector stages, one small file each, no particle system and no per-frame animation. Growth is a 240 ms cross-fade between two stage assets when a threshold is crossed, and nothing at all on every other launch — including on the low-end target device, where it must cost zero frames.

Wireframe — privacy & data

Grouped by the question a worried person actually asks: who can get in, who can see it, and how do I take it away. Not by technical subsystem.

The status card is a claim, so it must be true
It reads the actual transfer ledger. If backup or the assistant were ever enabled, the card changes to name what left, when, and to where — it does not stay green.
Every switch states its consequence
The secondary line is not a description of the setting, it is what happens to the user. “Sam isn't told why” is the important half of the pause control.
Delete is destructive and looks it
Red label, at the end, behind a dialog that requires typing the word and states plainly that there is no backup and no recovery. The cancel button holds focus. There is no retention offer, no “export first?” upsell, no survey.
The honesty note
The last block on the screen, permanent. A privacy-first product that overstates its protection is more dangerous than one that never claimed any — the note names the two threats a device lock genuinely does not cover.
No decoy mode
Considered and rejected for v1. A fake-calculator disguise gives real protection against a casual glance and none against a determined search, while creating a false sense of safety we cannot support and cannot test. If it ships later it ships with an explicit threat-model page.
06
Motion & performance

The reference device is a 2020 midrange Android: 4 × A55, 3 GB RAM, 60 Hz panel, no GPU headroom. Every rule below exists so that this phone never drops a frame the user can feel — and so the app still opens in under two seconds when it does.

Budgets
Cold start to Today< 1800 ms
Tab switch< 120 ms
Tap to visual feedback< 50 ms
Log write to “Saved”< 100 ms
Month recalculation< 16 ms
Steady-state memory< 180 MB
Animate only these two properties

transform and opacity. Both are compositor-only; neither triggers layout or paint. Anything that would animate width, height, top, left, colour, box-shadow, filter or border-radius is redesigned rather than optimised.

Screen push — translateX 24px + fade, 180 ms
Sheet — translateY, 240 ms, no scrim blur
Press — scale(0.97), 120 ms ease-out; releases on the same curve
Plant growth — cross-fade between two static assets
Confidence meter — fills once on mount, never loops
Reduced motion

When the OS setting is on, every transition becomes a ≤80 ms opacity change. Nothing translates, nothing scales. No animation is load-bearing for meaning, so nothing is lost — the confidence meter still shows three of five filled, it just does not fill.

Below 45 FPS sustained the app drops to the same path automatically and does not tell the user.

Rendering rules
· One month of calendar mounted at a time — no carousel, no off-screen prerender
· Insight and history lists virtualised past 40 rows
· Charts are static SVG paths computed once, not a charting runtime
· Icons are a subset font or inline paths, never per-icon network requests
· All estimate maths runs synchronously on write, never on render
· One font family (variable), subset to Latin — under 90 KB total
Banned outright

Backdrop blur · large or animated gradients · particle and confetti effects · autoplay video · full-bleed bitmap backgrounds · animated chart draw-on · parallax · shared-element transitions between tabs · unbounded scroll · any gesture more complex than tap, scroll and vertical drag · skeleton shimmer loops · haptics on anything but destructive confirmation.
